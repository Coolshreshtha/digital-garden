There are some patterns of behaviors, some roadblocks, that you keep running into. I have mine, like being late to things, and dealing with overwhelm (or at least have had these for the recent past).

One of biggest fears before coming to graduate school was that all the stability and structure I created in my sabbatical year would come crumbling down. I know that's not entirely true, because in some sense you never really "lose" progress, and it is always marginally easier to build a system the second time around than it was the first. But nevertheless, the fear and feeling of "falling back" is real and feels very demotivating and hurtful.

A month into grad school and I'm starting to run into my old dysfunction friends. The overwhelm, the resulting messiness and disorganization, feeling like life is rushing past you, and even failing to do the "basics" of human activities, like having meals and getting sleep. In some ways, I'm better off than I would have been in the past, because I know what is contributing to this pattern and have a toolkit for what has worked in the past. But despite all this knowledge, I'm not always able to translate it to action. 

It doesn't help that I don't have access to my biggest "pill" in the toolkit, affection from my inner-circle. This is true for everyone, but folks who have dealt with neuro-differences related dysfunction know how this can literally be a life-saver. I'm fortunate to be only a phone call away from my anchors, but it just isn't the same as being able to go them in person. 

But that is not to say all is lost. I find that recognizing where I am in the spiral certainly helps me prevent the situation from getting worse sometimes. This morning I intentionally planned to skip a class to give myself time to un-overwhelm and get my noisy brain to calm down. I have also taken multiple in-days (i often call them turtle days, because I go into my shell, only emerging for food and water), though it hasn't always been possible to take one every week.

I'm also working on building more enabling (rather than restrictive) structure into my day, so I don't have to rely on my already-scarce mental resources in times of need. I've had good success with budgets, and daily schedules in the past, as well as processes for frequent things and chains of tasks. Another area of focus is food and water (I've been TERRIBLE about this lately and it shows.) Speaking of, I'm gonna make a checklist now to keep a eye on all this!

(* I notice in overwhelming times, it can often be by the time you realise you are adrift from your own vibe. A manifesto will be reminder of what matters, and maybe even what is changing, as it inevitably does)
