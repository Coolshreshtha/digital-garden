I've just come to the end of my first week in Barcelona. 

A new city, a new continent, a new group of people, heck, even a new field of work!

I'm settling in well. Stayed at a hostel for a couple days before moving to my shared accommodation, I get my own room in a flat that I share with two other people. And, for the first time in a while, I have a balcony!

In a lot of ways, it feels like the beginning of something new and good. But like all new beginnings, it does bring into focus the contrast from what things were before (they were different, but not half bad). It makes me think about the choices I have made so far, and some that I might have to make in the future (but like I often say, [[worry doesn't have a pre-pay plan]]). 

Aside from the vague-ness of all that, it has been cool to learn a lot of new things. I've managed to pick up a few bits of Spanish and Catalan, and have had more than a taste of MATLAB and STATA. I can already tell it will be a fair bit of work coming up to speed with things and doing what the program asks of me, but hopefully it would be the kind of effort that makes you feel good at the end of it all!

Food has been a highlight so far. I often get chocolate milk at the school cafeteria, and the lunch and snacks feel like such a steal compared to US prices (I'm aware that they aren't such a good deal for locals who earn in the Spanish economy). 

I'm working on re-establishing some systems in my life, such as meal planning and stuff, and also for stuff like writing and other creative pursuits. With all the changes, I feel like rebuilding them will take some effort and some help, and thankfully we've got plenty of that!

I feel like these past few days in Spain have also made me think about what I want from life. Obviously thinking =/= having an answer yet, but thought I should mention it haha. 


Barcelona as a city definitely has a very interesting vibe. Right from the airplane, I could see that the city is really unlike anything I've experience before, the mountains and the build up city looked so beautiful and "foreign", and of course very beautiful. There were some hiccups at the airport in terms of getting a cab, because of a Catalan regulation that imposes a 15min wait before you can book a rideshare at the airport (to protect local taxi drivers). But once I managed to book one and was done circling the airport parking twice looking for my cab, I was treated to the most beautiful Barcelona landscapes on either side of the street. It really made me feel like I was in a real life game of geoguesser!

As we drove into the city, I got to see just how different, and yet familiar the city looks. There were gentle hills like San Francisco, clothes on the balcony like India, and vintage looking architecture like London. Yet, the energy of the city is not something I've seen before, but I can't exactly explain it. Maybe I'll try again someday when I'm more articulate.

You definitely need Spanish to fit into the city. Sure, most people are happy to switch to English when I sheepishly respond "Ingles" to whatever they said. But to truly gel with the city and remove that "outsider-ness", I would like to get to at least conversational in Spanish. I'm gonna save Catalan for later, because it is similar enough to Spanish to get super confusing and mess up my learning. 


Barcelona also does a lot of things right from an urban policy lens, such as their raised curbs next to bus stops, the diamond shaped intersections that really open up the space, and amazing trains. I think this helps with the open and alive vibes of the city. I went to a street fest the other day, and it is really cool how much civic sense everyone has. In contrast to Chicago street festivals, the music wasn't so loud that it hurt, people crowded around the stage for performances but did not block the sidewalk, and everyone seems to respect the neighborhood and doesn't turn it into a pile of trash.
