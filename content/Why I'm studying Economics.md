I'm getting a Masters in Economics of Public Policy. This isn't the most obvious choice after an undergrad in Computer Science and several years working in technology consulting. So I figured I should write more about my rationale for the move. Selfishly, this will also be a good reminder to look back and reflect how things and my own preferences evolve over time. 

A big reason is that, Economics is honestly just more interesting to me. The questions it asks is just more in line with how I am curious about the world. Just today, as I was exploring the stacks at UPF library, I notice so many books I would gladly pull off the shelf and read if I have time, like the how cities and their economic footprint shape each other, what role does family and marriage play from an economic lens, etc. I feel like this happens very often, like when I read Big Capital and how early Pakistan designed it's economic landscape, or like how medieval society in India was shaped by the social policies of the time. Even if I go back to my undergrad, I notice that my enthusiasm for technology and computing was less "ooh look at this new shiny piece of tech" and more "this is why it came to be, and this is why we use it today" or "this gadget has transformed our world in this manner". In hindsight, I'm curious how much of this Social Sciences leaning was shaped by Grinnell <3

There was also the pragmatic side of things. In the past year, I spent a lot of time exploring different career paths and where they might take me. Economics sits at that nice spot where I enjoy it enough to make an effort, and there is plenty of ways to adapt and do well in the world with this set of skills. It is also a space where I think my previous computing experience complements the new skills I'm going to acquire. I feel like my previous work showed me how to drive a car, and my current education will teach me when I should drive, how to navigate using a map, and even where I should drive to!

There is also an aspirational side to all this. For those who haven't had hours-long conversations with me about this, I often notice how my life is shaped by the decisions and policies of the different places I call home (for the better and for the "fun stories"). In the past few years, it was getting increasingly frustrating to feel like my time and life was going into an endless pit of meaninglessness. My naive hope in this career switch is that hopefully whatever I do now would fit better into how I see the world, even if it doesn't change the world. I feel like even the tiniest bit of work really compound over months and years, and hopefully my contribution would be a good one (or at least feel more fulfilling than the hours I spent in my previous work)

I'm approaching the year with an open mind and an open heart. But hopefully this time next year, I would have a nice set of new skills, some fancy jargon to throw around at parties, a good group of friends, and maybe a new refined lens of looking at the world (wouldn't mind it if you throw in a nice job with that ;))
