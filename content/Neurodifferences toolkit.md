I find ADHD as a word to be a misnomer. For me, a more accurate descriptor would be Attention, Emotion and Executive Function Dysregulation Syndrome, or AEEFDS, will work on a catchier acronym. 

Anyway, here is a few things that I think benefit all folks with different flavors of neurodifferences

*  Reminder to drop the shame with all AEEFDS issues. Sure, it breaks societal norms and sure it would be nice to get these things right. But feeling shame and negative emotions around it helps no one. I find rephrasing it as a resource issue and brainstorming how to work around it to be more helpful and more likely to stick. 
* Self-compassion:  I recently heard this metaphor on a podcast (will add which one at some point). It describes the day to day uncertainty of your experience as seasons. Some days you would excel at all the expectations, some days the winds of emotions are too strong to make any progress. Some days need additional layers of comfort and caffiene to get through lack of focus. I honestly think this is a great mindset for anyone, but especially so for folks with neurodifferences. You don't find the weather gods, you just make sure to choose your outfit for the weather, and plan accordingly. Just like you dont plan an hour long walk in a park on a -15 C day, maybe move things around to adapt to your emotional weather for the day.
* Aim for structure, not consistency: I notice, all allures of streaks and consistency eventually fades for me. I find the best structures to be those that give me a sense of direction, especially when I inevitably get off track. ANother key point to note here is that, the intent and goal at the base of this structure has to be personally meaningful and intrinsically motivating to me.
