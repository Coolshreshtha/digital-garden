Immigration has been hugely beneficial for me, and this seems to be the case across the board as people who immigrate have better financial outcomes than their peer groups back home (Remind me to cite the paper here). 

But in the midst of all this, lets not forget that a lot of immigration policy actively works to hinder us. In a world where one's best chance of growth is by being adaptible and switch fast, restrictive immigration policy isn't just annoying, it's actively harmful. 

If we consider the education restriction for H1B as an example, the requirement to work only in spaces directly related to your education hinders any side hustles and projects that could be very fulfiling (monetarily and otherwise) but put you at the risk of violating your status. On the other hand, their is a whole parallel industry of extortionist "consultancies" whose entire job is to produce fake compliance paperwork for people working on their side hustles. It is so widespread that it is hard to believe the immigration authorities of the world aren't aware of it.

On my recent trip to London, every fast service restaurant had indian employees who had right to work post grad. One can't help but think that this is how home office is killing two birds with one stone, by using international students first to bankroll their unis, and then to kill the employee shortage in fast-food restaurants, without offering a separate option for the latter (and dealing with the barrage of applications that would bring). Given the burn-and-churn nature of this industry, it wouldn't be a huge loss when their post-study-work-visa eventually expires, as a new lot of fresh grads would be ready to take their spots, waiting for their "right jobs". 

The path to any career today is trying a bunch of things and seeing what sticks. This demands creativity, transferable skills, and perhaps most imporantly, a legal status that affords you the "priviledge" to try things out. Let's see how other successful immigrants of today thread this needle.
